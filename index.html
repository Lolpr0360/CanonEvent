ADMIT ONE — PARTY OF 4–5

Canon Event

You're not an elf. You're not a fighter. You're you, dragged sideways into a story that isn't playing by one genre's rules — somewhere fantasy and sci-fi (and who knows what else) start bleeding into each other. Your body is tracked zone by zone instead of one HP bar, and everything — a fight, a lie, a locked door — settles with a Rock-Paper-Scissors Clash.

4–5 PlayersPlay yourselves, literally
All RPSEvery roll retired — Clashes only
Body ZonesNo HP bar, no death saves

The Core Mechanic

Resolution: The Clash Engine

Everything resolves with Rock-Paper-Scissors against the GM — no dice anywhere. Non-combat moments use a single Event Clash. Combat splits into an Attack sequence and a Defence sequence, below.

Brawn

Physical action — fights, chases, feats of strength, anything your body has to do.

Brains

Puzzles, tech, lore, magic-logic, reading a room's hidden mechanics.

Bravado

Charm, style, bluffing, performance — making people feel something on purpose.

Non-Combat Actions — The Event Clash

Every non-combat contested moment — a lie, a locked door, a first date with a sentient hologram — resolves with a single RPS throw against the GM.

Event Clash
You winSuccess — full effect.
DrawStalemate or a second chance — the GM's call, depending on the tier below: either nothing changes and the tension holds, or you get a follow-up throw with a complication attached.
You loseFailure — the tier's consequence applies.

In a combat situation, before the throw, the GM can be asked the tier— it tells you how forgiving a Draw is and how much a Loss costs.

TierUsed forOn a DrawOn a Loss
Quick BeatFlavor actions, minor checksStalemate, no costShrug it off, minor cost
ChallengeStandard obstacles, most scenesSecond chance, minor complication attachedA real setback or cost
ShowdownSet-pieces, mini-bossesSecond chance, but a real complication attachedMajor complication or harm
Legendary ClashThe finale, the big badStalemate holds — no second chances hereSession-defining defeat

Combat — Attack & Defence

No dice in a fight. Combat splits into two different sequences depending on whether you're swinging or being swung at — each uses RPS against the GM. Note: Enemies attack together

When you attack

Throw 1 — The Outcome Clash
You winHit connects clean — full damage based on weapon type.
DrawThe enemy blocks, but takes partial damage — Tier 1, 'Tis But a Scratch.
You loseThe enemy counters or dodges — the GM's call. On a counter, their attack triggers a defensive RPS.

Throw 2 still applies exactly as below — run it whenever a hit actually landed (a Win, or the partial damage on a Draw). Skip it if the enemy fully dodges.

Throw 2 — The Location Clash
You winYou call the location outright.
DrawIt lands on the zone adjacent to the one you called.
You loseThe strike goes wild and never reaches the target — whoever swung takes a Tier 1 Wince themselves instead.

Adjacency: Torso is adjacent to everything (both arms, both legs, head, wings, tail). Every limb's only neighbor is Torso.

On purpose, not a bug: you can win Throw 1 clean and still lose Throw 2. That's a real hit that goes wrong in the follow-through — you connected, but the finish is yours to eat instead.

When you defend

A single throw, no Throw 2 — for now, the GM narrates where an incoming hit lands.

Defence Clash
You winYour choice: Counter Attack (Imeddiately get an attack RPS or GMs decision on outcome) or Dodge (you take no damage).
DrawYou stand your ground and take the hit — a shield or Enhancement on that zone absorbs it first, if you have one.
You loseYou get hit — full Damage, GM's call on location.

Weapon Type & damage

The GM sets an attack's Weapon type before the Clash — Tier 1: blunt and light; Tier 2: Blunt and heavy; Tier 3: Sharp and Light; Tier 4: Sharp and Heavy; Tier 5: Projectile or just Sharp with pure luck See Body Zones for the full Wince Scale, and Enhancements for how gear soaks a hit before the zone does.

Multiple zones at once: an explosion, a combo, or a kaiju swipe can hit several zones in one action. The GM names every zone affected and runs the appropriate Clash(es) for each — or, for speed against mooks, just narrates the spread directly without extra throws.

Showdown Clashes

The same Event Clash, run player-versus-player instead of against the GM — for face-to-face beats between two named characters: banter battles, arm wrestling, staring contests, a villain's monologue duel.

Edge Tokens

Edge Moves aren't picked once and locked in — they're assigned by the GM, Zone by Zone, based on what actually happened to your character.

How assignment works: at the start of each Zone, the GM looks back at what a character did and went through in the last one — their actions, wins, losses, close calls — and assigns them the Edge category that fits the story. That's their Edge Move until the next Zone.
Challenging the Assignment: if a player thinks the GM's pick doesn't fit, they can challenge it on the spot. That sends it to a vote — the rest of the table decides whether the assignment sticks. Majority rules.
Edge MoveWhat it does
ADHDCopy the last throw you made against this same opponent, no new throw needed.
SchizophreniaAsk the GM one question about the scene before you interact — their answer may or may not be true.
DepressionIf you lose two Clashes in a row, your next loss automatically counts as a Win.
KleptomaniaDouble the outcome of your throw.
AnxietyRedo your throw once, and keep the new result.
angagnangnagnnagCopy the last throw you made in this same situation — works on a repeat scenario, not just a repeat opponent.
NormalOnce per session, borrow one Edge Token from each other player at the table.

Health & Turn Order

Body Zones

No HP bar, no initiative roll, no death saving throws. A body is six zones, each with its own small pool, and turn order runs on who's got the spotlight, not a die roll.

Spotlight Order (no initiative throw)

The GM opens the scene with whoever's action kicked it off. When a character's turn ends, that player points to who goes next — any PC or named NPC. Mooks don't get individual turns; the GM narrates their whole wave as one beat after each full round.

The Wince Scale

Which zone gets hit is decided by the Location Clash in Resolution. How much it costs is decided by this scale — apply the tier that matches the attack's weapon type on a Win, or Tier 1 on a Draw.

Multiple zones at once: an explosion, a combo, or a kaiju swipe can hit several zones in one action. The GM names every zone affected and runs a Location Clash for each — or, for speed against mooks, just narrates the spread directly.
The Fade Clock — replacing death saves: when Torso hits 0, the character is Downed with a 3-round clock running. Any ally can spend their turn on an Event Clash to stabilize them. If nobody manages it in time, that character is Written Out of the Episode — hurt, offscreen, back next session — rather than dead outright.
Optional Harsh Mode: if your table wants real stakes, Players can be executed by enemies to be perma-killed.

Gear

Enhancements

Enhancements are items that guard a specific body part and take damage before you do. Players only ever see the flavor — the real numbers live with the GM.

1 · It absorbs first

Wince damage to a covered body part hits the item's hidden capacity before it hits you.

2 · It breaks visibly

Once its hidden capacity is spent, the GM narrates the break — a strap snapping, a screen splitting. That's the only signal players get.

3 · No numbers, ever

Players get a name and a vague sentence. Capacity, coverage, and break conditions stay on the GM's side.

A worked example of the split — what the player sees, versus what the GM actually tracks. "Heavier than it looks. You're not sure why you still wear it."

GM Eyes Only
Covers: Weapon arm only.
Reduces every Wince tier to that arm by one step, permanently, no capacity to run out.
Break cue: none — it's built to last, which is its own kind of story hook.
Making your own: pick a body part it covers, a hidden capacity or reduction, and a break cue you'll actually remember to narrate. Then write the player one honest-sounding but non-numeric sentence about it. That's the whole item.

Currencies

Fandom Points & Crossover Chips

Two meta-currencies keep the tone light and the crossovers rewarding.

Fandom Points

Earned for a landed pun, a good reference, playing your Flaw, or a genuinely funny bit. The GM hands these out on the spot. Other Players can vote on it but GM call is final.

Spend 1 to: cancel a loss into a draw (Plot Armor), ask the GM a META question (secretly), or cash in a callback for a free Edge Token.

Crossover Chips

Earned when the party combines tools or tropes from two different Zones in one clever move.

Spend 1 to: borrow another Zone's signature power for a scene, or punch a shortcut portal straight between two Zones.
